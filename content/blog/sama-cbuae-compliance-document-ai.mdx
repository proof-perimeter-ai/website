SAMA and CBUAE compliance for document AI splits into two regulators asking different questions at different points in the vendor relationship. The Saudi Central Bank's Cloud Computing Framework gates the decision before it's made — a member organization needs SAMA's approval before it can even sign a cloud contract, not just prove good behavior afterward. The Central Bank of the UAE takes an architectural approach instead: a bank's "master system of record" has to stay resident in the UAE continuously, regardless of where processing happens, and its February 2026 AI Guidance Note now layers model-governance duties on top of that. Neither framework treats a document AI vendor's marketing claim about "regional hosting" as sufficient on its own. This guide walks through what each requires, where they diverge, and what a procurement team in Riyadh, Jeddah, Abu Dhabi, or Dubai needs in writing before a KYC packet or loan file ever reaches a model.

## What Are SAMA and CBUAE, and Why Do They Cover Document AI Vendors?

SAMA and CBUAE are the two primary financial regulators in the Gulf's largest markets, and both treat outsourced technology — including AI — as a supervised extension of the regulated institution, not a vendor's private concern. SAMA covers commercial banks, insurers, finance companies, payment service providers, and credit bureaus in Saudi Arabia; CBUAE covers banks, insurers, exchange houses, finance companies, and payment service providers licensed in the UAE. A document AI platform that classifies, extracts, or reads a KYC packet, loan file, or claims bundle on a regulated institution's behalf falls inside both regulators' outsourcing and technology-risk perimeters the moment it touches a document — there's no carve-out because the function runs on a model instead of a person.

## What Does SAMA's Cloud Computing Framework Require Before a Contract Is Even Signed?

SAMA's Cyber Security Framework folds cloud computing into its broader risk domains, and the cloud-specific subdomain (CSF 3.4.3) is where most document AI procurement decisions actually live or die.

### The Pre-Approval Requirement Most Regimes Don't Have

Clause CSF 3.4.3.4(a)(2) states that a member organization "should obtain SAMA approval prior to using cloud services or signing the contract with the cloud provider" — a requirement that applies to hybrid and public cloud arrangements regardless of whether the data counts as material or critical. That's a materially different gate than DORA, MAS, or RBI's outsourcing frameworks, all of which govern what the *contract* has to contain once signed. SAMA's rule sits earlier, which means a document AI vendor evaluation in Saudi Arabia needs regulatory lead time most procurement timelines don't budget for.

### Data Classification Decides What Can Leave the Kingdom

SAMA's framework requires institutions to classify data by sensitivity, and the highest tiers — customer PII, account balances, transaction histories, payment credentials, and credit decisions — carry an in-Kingdom hosting expectation regardless of whether the institution runs its own data center or a public cloud region. A KYC packet or loan file sits squarely in that top tier: the fields a document AI model extracts (income figures, account numbers, identity data) are exactly what the scheme is built to keep inside Saudi borders.

## What Does CBUAE's Outsourcing Regulation Require for Data Held Outside the UAE?

The UAE takes a different mechanical approach to the same underlying concern: rather than gating the initial contracting decision, CBUAE's [Outsourcing Regulation for Banks](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/en/rulebook/article-6-outsourcing-outside-uae) requires an always-current, in-country copy of the data a document AI vendor would otherwise process abroad.

### The Master System of Record Has to Stay in the UAE

Article 6 requires banks to continuously maintain and store within the UAE a "master system of record" — the collection of all data, including confidential data, required to conduct the bank's core activities, manage risk, and meet legal and regulatory obligations. A document AI vendor that processes a KYC packet or claims file entirely outside the UAE doesn't just need a data-residency clause; the institution needs a live, in-country record of that same data regardless of where the vendor's inference runs — shifting the question from "where does the vendor host" to "does the bank's own in-UAE record stay current."

### Confidential Data Needs Regulatory Approval and Customer Consent to Leave

The same article bars banks from sharing customers' confidential data outside the UAE without both CBUAE's prior approval and the customer's written consent, and it prohibits arrangements with providers domiciled in jurisdictions that can't offer the same data protection the UAE requires. A vendor reselling inference through an offshore frontier-model API has to clear that two-part consent bar for every document carrying customer data — not a one-time assurance, but a chain a bank has to produce on request.

## What Does CBUAE's February 2026 AI Guidance Note Add Beyond Outsourcing?

On February 23, 2026, CBUAE issued its [Guidance Note on the Consumer Protection and Responsible Adoption and Use of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning by Licensed Financial Institutions](https://www.centralbank.ae/media/mykn5fue/cbuae-issues-guidance-note-to-protect-consumers-and-ensure-responsible-use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-the-financial-sector-en.pdf) — supplementing, not replacing, the outsourcing rules above and CBUAE's 2022 Model Management Standards. It requires every licensed institution to run a documented AI governance framework proportionate to its size, hold board and senior management directly accountable for AI outcomes, and embed security- and privacy-by-design into every AI system it deploys or relies on through a vendor. For a document AI platform, that means the institution now has to show board-level accountability for a model's extraction decisions, not just a data-processing agreement — a governance layer on top of Article 6's residency mechanics, the same way MAS's AI risk consultation and DORA's human-oversight provisions layer onto their own outsourcing frameworks.

## How Does SAMA and CBUAE Compliance for Document AI Actually Work in Practice?

The practical difference is *when* each regulator asks its question. SAMA's pre-approval clause is a gate at signing — a vendor either has a Saudi institution's contracting path cleared before deployment, or the arrangement can't legally start. CBUAE's master-system-of-record requirement is a standing architectural condition — it doesn't stop a contract from being signed, but it means an institution maintains in-country data parity for as long as the arrangement runs, independent of where the vendor's model executes. A vendor built around a resold frontier-model API can, in principle, satisfy either regime with enough contractual engineering. What it can't easily do is satisfy SAMA's pre-approval timeline and CBUAE's continuous-parity requirement at once while also producing the audit trail both frameworks eventually ask for — because the underlying architecture, not the paperwork, determines whether that evidence exists. ## A Practical Gulf Readiness Checklist for Document AI Procurement

- **Build SAMA's pre-approval step into the procurement timeline**, not after it — CSF 3.4.3.4(a)(2) requires clearance before the cloud contract is signed.
- **Ask which data-classification tier the document type falls into** under SAMA's framework, and confirm hosting matches what that tier requires.
- **Confirm the institution's own UAE master system of record stays current** if the vendor's processing happens elsewhere — CBUAE's Article 6 makes this the bank's obligation, not just the vendor's.
- **Get CBUAE approval and customer consent in writing** before any confidential data leaves the UAE, for every document type the vendor touches.
- **Ask how the vendor documents board-level AI accountability**, not just data handling — CBUAE's February 2026 guidance note expects governance evidence an outsourcing contract alone doesn't cover.
- **Check whether [SOC 2 controls](/glossary/soc-2-document-controls) and [data residency](/glossary/data-residency-in-document-ai) commitments cover inference, not just storage** — both Gulf frameworks reach further than either document typically shows on its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does SAMA's pre-approval requirement apply to every cloud-based document AI arrangement, or only large ones?

It applies broadly. CSF 3.4.3.4(a)(2) requires SAMA approval before using cloud services or signing the cloud contract, and that requirement isn't conditioned on the arrangement being classified as material — a smaller-scale document AI pilot still needs to clear it before deployment.

### Can a document AI vendor satisfy CBUAE's Article 6 by hosting data in the UAE but processing it abroad?

Not fully. Article 6 requires the bank to maintain its own master system of record within the UAE regardless of where a vendor's processing happens, and separately restricts sharing confidential data outside the UAE without CBUAE approval and customer consent — a vendor that only commits to UAE data storage leaves both requirements partly unmet.

### Is CBUAE's February 2026 AI Guidance Note a replacement for its existing outsourcing rules?

No. The Guidance Note explicitly supplements, rather than replaces, CBUAE's existing Outsourcing Regulation and its 2022 Model Management Standards. A financial institution has to satisfy the outsourcing and data-residency requirements and the AI-governance expectations the new guidance adds.

## The Takeaway

SAMA and CBUAE compliance for document AI means clearing two differently timed gates, not one regional checklist. SAMA asks its question before a contract exists — approval has to be secured ahead of signing, gated by where the data sits in its own classification scheme. CBUAE asks its question continuously, through a UAE-resident master system of record and, since February 2026, board-level accountability for how an AI system is governed. A platform that runs inference inside the institution's own perimeter answers both timelines with the same architecture, rather than negotiating pre-approval and residency parity separately for every Gulf market it serves.
