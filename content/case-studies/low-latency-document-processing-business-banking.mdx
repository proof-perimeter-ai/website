Here's how the engineering team shipped real-time document upload in their onboarding flow, delighting users and saving weeks of engineering effort.

## The challenge

The variety of documents onboarding candidates submit is huge, arriving in dozens of languages — non-Latin alphabets, right-to-left text, and wildly different formats across hundreds of countries. Any approach relying solely on traditional OCR couldn't handle the long tail of edge cases in a truly global document set.

p90 latency also had to be fast enough for a real-time product experience: onboarding is the first thing a user experiences, and it shapes how they see the entire product. If document verification is slow, the user is stuck staring at a spinner, unable to move forward.

## Evaluating every option on the market

- **Cloud providers.** The team already used a major cloud provider for most of its stack, so that provider's OCR service was the obvious first try — but performance didn't hold up against the diversity of document types.
- **Direct to foundation models.** Foundation model APIs worked at first, but performance issues, high latency, and the lack of bounding boxes eventually made them unsuitable for production. Bounding boxes matter a lot when processing sensitive financial data and needing source citations for every extracted field, powering efficient downstream human-review workflows.
- **Other startups.** Some didn't clear the required accuracy threshold; others were API-only and lacked the tooling needed for production.
- **Proof Perimeter.** Advanced vision-language models showed state-of-the-art accuracy on varied document layouts. The team could toggle between a fast mode enabling a sub-7-second p90 and a performance mode for async jobs. Bounding box coordinates and citations enabled an intuitive user experience embedded directly in the product, and schema optimization tooling removed the need for manual prompt engineering while built-in eval tooling prevented regressions as new edge cases emerged.
